Warning Callout Component - Basic Examples

Use warning callouts to help users identify and understand important warning content on the page, even if they do not read the whole page.

Basic Warning Callout

Important: School, nursery or work

Stay away from school, nursery or work until all the spots have crusted over. This is usually 5 days after the spots appeared.

  • Difficulty breathing

Do not wait if symptoms worsen. Seek immediate medical attention.

Size Variants

Compact Warning Callout

Important: Limited time offer

This compact callout takes up less space while still drawing attention to important information.

Critical Warning Callout

Critical: Urgent action required

This critical variant provides enhanced visual prominence for the most important warnings that require immediate attention.

Subtle Warning Callout

Note: Please note

This subtle variant is less prominent but still clearly indicates important information that users should be aware of.

In Context Usage

Vaccination Information

Getting vaccinated is one of the most important steps you can take to protect yourself and your family from serious diseases.

Important: Allergic reactions

Tell your healthcare provider if you have had severe allergic reactions to any vaccines in the past. They will monitor you closely after vaccination.

Who should get vaccinated?

Most people can safely receive vaccines. However, there are some exceptions and precautions to consider.

Side effects

Most people experience only mild side effects after vaccination, such as soreness at the injection site or mild fatigue.

Note: Keep records

Keep a record of all your vaccinations. You may need this information for travel, school, or work requirements.

Usage Guidelines

✅ When to use warning callouts

  • Information that is time critical
  • Content that could significantly impact the user's health or wellbeing
  • To address common misconceptions
  • When users might miss important information in body text
  • To highlight exceptions to general rules

✅ Best practices

  • Give the callout a short, clearly worded heading
  • Make the callout concise, specific and self-contained
  • Avoid having more than 2 callouts on a page
  • Use appropriate heading levels that fit the page hierarchy
  • Always include the visually hidden prefix for screen readers
  • Use role="text" to ensure screen readers read the heading as one unit

❌ When not to use

  • On transactional pages (use other components instead)
  • For general information that isn't critical or time-sensitive
  • To replace proper page structure or clear content organization
  • For promotional content or calls to action
  • When the information can be better presented in the main content

❌ Don't

  • Introduce a callout with "If this happens..." - explain the circumstances
  • Use more than 2 warning callouts on a single page
  • Rely on color alone to convey the warning
  • Use vague or unclear headings
  • Forget the visually hidden prefix for screen readers
